    Resources

    Everything on this site comes from the Maui Nui Landscape Conservation Plan, published by the Hawaiʻi Division of Forestry and Wildlife (DOFAW). Here you can download the full plan and a map file of the conservation footprints, find out how to cite the plan, and browse the published work and existing plans it draws on.

      The plan covers the 442 native species at risk of extinction in Maui Nui, where to recover them, which actions will do the most good, and how to track progress over a 20-year implementation period.plan p.iv-v, Executive Summary

      Conservation footprint map file (KMZ)

      A Google Earth (KMZ) file of the conservation footprints will be offered here once DOFAW approves its release.

      The file will show the recovery footprints for each taxonomic group and open in Google Earth and most GIS programs. To explore the footprints online, use the Conservation Footprints map.

      Footprints are living products and are updated in the Adaptive Conservation Planner. The file will be a dated snapshot; the official, current footprints are kept in the Conservation Planner.plan p.A5-1 to A5-2 Partners with an ACP account can export current footprint data as CSV, GeoPackage, or GeoJSON.plan p.A5-1

      Related plans and guidance

      The MNLCP does not replace existing plans. Hawaiʻi already has many high-quality plans, but they are not integrated at the landscape or management level, and managers must reconcile dozens of documents with different assumptions and scales. The MNLCP brings them together into one landscape-scale strategy.plan p.iv; p.3-4 Ch 1a

      The plan builds on:

      • Species recovery plans. Each at-risk species' recovery target is based on abundance, distribution, and viability criteria derived from existing recovery plans where they exist, supplemented by expert input.plan p.7 Ch 2a; p.3-4 Ch 1a; p.18 Ch 4c; p.49 Ch 6d(iv)
      • The Hawaiʻi State Wildlife Action Plan (SWAP). One of the sources used to identify Maui Nui's at-risk species.plan p.16 Ch 4a; Terms p.121
      • Site-specific management plans and broader strategic frameworks.plan p.3 Ch 1a
      • Recovery goals from the Hawaiʻi and Pacific Plant Recovery Coordinating Committee (HPPRCC). Standardized recovery goals used, with recovery plans and expert review, to set plant recovery targets.plan p.18 Ch 4c; p.49 Ch 6d(iv)
      • Species status assessments, recovery strategies, action plans, and management plans, used by the action prioritization effort.plan p.13 Ch 3d
      • Hawaiian hoary bat guidance. Hawaiian Hoary Bat Guidance for Wind Energy Project Habitat Conservation Plans (ESRC and DOFAW 2024) and the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service 5-Year Status Review for the ʻōpeʻapeʻa (2021).plan p.115, p.119 Literature Cited
      • The Hawaiʻi Gap Analysis Program (HIGAP), an earlier species-based conservation planning effort that informed how the plan describes the management landscape (Gon et al. 2006).plan p.92 Ch 7e

      Coming next

      • Managers' Handbook. A practical guide for DOFAW staff using MNLCP tools and outputs, being developed under the Implementation Team.plan p.15 Ch 3f; p.A5-2

      Maui Nui Landscape Conservation Plan

      A landscape-scale roadmap to prevent extinctions, recover at-risk species and restore native ecosystems across Maui, Molokaʻi, Lānaʻi and Kahoʻolawe. Led by the Hawaiʻi Division of Forestry and Wildlife.

      Plan Version September 29, 2026. Subject to periodic review and revision.
