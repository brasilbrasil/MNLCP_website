    Putting It to Work

    Adaptive Conservation Planner & Tools

    The MNLCP is not only a document. It is delivered as a living system, and at its center is the Adaptive Conservation Planner, an online mapping tool that holds the plan's conservation footprints and the species, habitat, and threat information behind them. Managers use it to build annual work plans and to update priorities as conditions, knowledge, and funding change. A second tool, Find My Microhabitat, helps field teams find the best spots on the ground within a priority area, and three more tools are planned.

      What the Adaptive Conservation Planner is

      The Adaptive Conservation Planner (ACP, or "Conservation Planner" for short) is where the MNLCP's final spatial products live.plan p.12 Ch 3c; Terms p.120 It holds the recovery footprints for each taxonomic group, the planning units (PUs) that make them up, the species targeted in each unit, and supporting information on species, habitats, and threats.

      It is part of the TFE-SCP process, the comprehensive planning process used for the plan's spatial prioritization. The ACP carries conservation footprints from draft prioritization results, through fine-tuning by species experts, and into long-term care of the plan.plan p.A5-1

      A conservation plan should not be a static document that goes out of date. The Conservation Planner is how the MNLCP stays current.plan p.A5-1

      Who it is for

      The ACP is a working tool for the people who carry out the plan: DOFAW staff, taxon experts, and land managers. It is not a public web map and it is not a task tracker.plan p.A5-1 Users sign in with an account, and what each person can do depends on their role:

      • Read-only users can view footprints, map layers, and species targets.
      • Users can also propose footprint edits and write notes.
      • Admins review and approve proposed edits and manage accounts.ACP user guide, docs/site/roles-and-permissions.qmd. Not in the plan; the plan says only "authorized users" (p.A5-1).

      Sensitive information, such as exact locations of rare species, is shown only to accounts that have been given permission for it.ACP user guide, roles-and-permissions.qmd

      What you can do with it

      Plan the year's work

      The plan describes the ACP as the tool managers use to develop annual work plans, adjust strategies based on results and new information, and explore different management scenarios.plan p.13 Ch 3c(ii); Terms p.120 For example, managers can narrow a priority footprint to the species in greatest need, such as plants facing imminent extinction that are known from only one or a few planning units, to set a focused first round of work.plan p.27 Ch 5e See Using the Plan for the full worked example.

      View footprints by group or species

      Choose one or more taxonomic groups and the ACP draws their conservation footprints on the map, separately or overlaid.plan p.A5-1 With several groups loaded, you can show a combined footprint, or a target richness map that colors each planning unit by how many at-risk species are targeted there. The richness view helps find exceptional areas that support many at-risk species or species facing imminent extinction.plan p.83 Ch 6i(iv), Figure 20; ACP user guide map-layers.qmd

      Search for a single species to see its suitable habitat, its recovery and priority units, and its known populations.ACP user guide species-layers.qmd

      See what is targeted in each planning unit

      Select a planning unit to see which species are targeted there, their habitat suitability, the evidence that they occur there, and whether their targets are met.plan p.A5-1

      Add context layers

      Overlay other maps, such as biodiversity reserves, fire risk, or climate resilience, to see how a footprint relates to land status and threats. Users can upload their own layers as well.ACP user guide map-layers.qmd; layer names from screenshots manage-aux-layers.png, map-layers.png

      Propose changes and add notes

      Authorized users can propose edits: move a species target to a different planning unit, add a new target location, remove a target, or change whether a target is part of the priority footprint. They can also attach notes to species or planning units to record field observations, access concerns, land-tenure information, or revisions needed later.plan p.A5-1

      Export the data

      Download footprint data as CSV, GeoPackage, or GeoJSON for use in GIS or other planning systems. Exports can be limited to selected groups, species, or planning units.plan p.A5-1; ACP user guide exports.qmd

      How the ACP keeps the plan current

      The ACP keeps two things side by side:plan p.A5-1 to A5-2

      • A versioned official footprint: a stable, dated snapshot (for example, the 2026 official footprint) used for reporting, hand-off, and as the formal reference for implementation.
      • A living platform: where ongoing edits, annual reviews, newly found populations, and changes in management conditions are added, with a record of how and why each change was made.

      This means DOFAW can give partners a stable product to plan around while keeping the footprints up to date as knowledge improves. Future annual reviews can change specific planning units, and the change history stays traceable.plan p.A5-2

      Changes are never made in one step. A proposed edit is saved privately, then published for review by the team, and only becomes part of the footprint once an administrator approves it.plan p.A5-1; ACP user guide edits-and-review.qmd

      Current status

      The Conservation Planner is in operational use. It has been used to fine-tune the plant recovery footprint (its most mature use), to review snail exclosure (kīpuka kāhuli) sites, and to review arthropod targets. It handles footprints for several taxonomic groups at once and has been tested through full edit, review, and approval cycles.plan p.A5-2

      Planned improvements include:plan p.A5-2 - better display of occurrence data for some groups - automatic detection of conflicting edits between recovery and priority footprints - custom overlays for individual species - clearer save and publish behavior - documentation so the system can be used beyond Maui Nui

      How the ACP fits with DOFAW's other systemsDetails

      The long-term design pairs two connected systems. The Conservation Planner handles the specialized footprint logic of the TFE-SCP process: species targets, criteria, notes, and footprint versions. DOFAW's Esri environment handles broader implementation tracking: field surveys, work orders, fence status, and effort records. The Conservation Planner is meant to complement Esri-based field systems, not replace them, by adding the species-level, multi-criteria footprint logic that standard GIS platforms do not provide.plan p.A5-2

      The ACP also shows recovery footprints, priority footprints, and narrower implementation subsets nested in a single view, so that a change made once carries through to every product that depends on it.plan p.A5-1

      Other tools

      The plan lists four companion tools. Only Find My Microhabitat is available now; the others will be released in the near future.plan p.A5-1 Introduction

      Find My Microhabitat (available)

      Conservation footprints answer the question "where should we focus?" at the scale of planning units, which can be hundreds of hectares. But an outplanting site, survey transect, or exclosure covers a fraction of a hectare. Find My Microhabitat bridges that gap: within a selected planning unit, it combines high-resolution data on vegetation structure, canopy cover, topographic position, moisture, and other local habitat variables to find the most suitable microsites for target species.plan p.A5-3

      It is useful for: - Outplanting: finding the spots within a footprint planning unit where rare plant reintroductions are most likely to succeed - Exclosure siting: refining candidate kīpuka kāhuli (snail exclosure) locations within priority areas - Survey targeting: sending field surveys to the most promising microsites for data-deficient species - Restoration planning: finding where habitat restoration will most benefit target speciesplan p.A5-3

      Plant Extinction Prevention Program (PEPP) partners already use the tool to choose outplanting sites within plant footprint planning units. It is being documented so that more DOFAW staff and partners can use it without developer support.plan p.A5-3 to A5-4 The plan gives no public link for the tool.

      Coming soon

      • Potential Recovery Sites (PRS): map layers of potential recovery sites based on climate suitability and habitat quality, to help managers focus field searches for suitable sites.plan p.53 Ch 6d(v); p.A5-1, A5-4
      • Climate Vectors
      • Footprint Summarizerplan p.A5-1, A5-4 (App 5 gives section headings only; Climate Vectors and Footprint Summarizer have no description in the plan)

      The plan says these within-unit tools will let the Conservation Planner show managers where species occur, where habitat quality is high, where potential recovery sites are, where climatic conditions seem to be changing most rapidly, and where certain microhabitats might be found within a planning unit.plan p.29-30 Ch 5f
