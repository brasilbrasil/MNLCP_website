## Lead
Three native seabirds that feed at sea but nest in Maui Nui's mountains are at risk: the ʻuaʻu, ʻaʻo and ʻakēʻakē. None is facing imminent extinction, but introduced predators, especially rats, mongoose and cats, have greatly reduced their numbers, along with habitat loss. The plan's strategy is to build predator-proof fences, control predators, manage nesting habitat and care for injured birds. It also aims to reduce deaths from night lighting, power lines, wind turbines and roads.

## Status and trends
Introduced mammalian predators, especially rats, mongoose and cats, together with habitat loss, have greatly reduced the abundance of Maui Nui seabirds. <!-- src: plan p.37, Ch 6b i --> Seabirds have a fairly low chance of persisting with no conservation action, but current conservation efforts already give them, along with Waterbirds, better than average persistence. <!-- src: plan p.40, Ch 6b vii -->

## Threats
- **Introduced predators.** Rats, mongoose and cats prey on nesting seabirds. <!-- src: plan p.37 -->
- **Habitat loss.** Loss of nesting habitat has contributed to the decline. <!-- src: plan p.37 -->
- **Infrastructure.** Artificial lights at night, power lines, wind turbines and roads harm seabird survival. <!-- src: plan p.37 -->

## Emergency actions
No seabirds are currently considered to face an imminent threat of extinction, so no emergency actions are listed for this group. <!-- src: plan p.38, Ch 6b iv; p.21, Table 2 -->

## Recovery strategy

### Goals
The plan aims to roughly double ʻuaʻu breeding pairs and to raise ʻakēʻakē and ʻaʻo numbers by an order of magnitude. <!-- src: plan p.38, Ch 6b v --> The targets are spread across East Maui, West Maui, Molokaʻi, Lānaʻi and Kahoʻolawe.

### Recovery footprint and priority footprint
Seabirds followed the plan's expert-led pathway. Like the Forest Bird Team, the Seabird Team used published literature, recovery plans and professional expertise to map the recovery footprint needed to sustain seabird populations on all four Maui Nui islands. <!-- src: plan p.39, Ch 6b vi --> Offshore islets are part of the recovery footprint, even where they are too small to show on the map. A subset of planning units (areas of about 2 km² used to organize the plan's maps) was chosen as the priority area for conservation action. <!-- src: plan p.39; planning unit size p.52 --> In the plan's map, the priority area and the initial implementation area are the same. <!-- src: plan p.39, Figure 7 caption -->

## Priority conservation actions
The plan's action prioritization used a modified Priority Threat Management (PTM) process. PTM estimates how much each of 27 strategies would raise the chance that each species group persists over 20 years. <!-- src: plan p.84, Ch 6j --> Like Forest birds, Seabirds gain the most from the bird-focused combination strategy S27. <!-- src: plan p.40 --> Experts ranked predator control actions, such as fencing and predator removal, as the actions to do first. Programs that rescue injured birds, and habitat management, are also high priorities. <!-- src: plan p.40 -->

|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | S10 | New predator-proof fencing (against rodents, mongoose and cats) | 48 |
| 2 | S7 | Invasive vertebrate predator control (rats, cats, dogs, mongoose, barn owls, cattle egrets, rabbits) | 46 |
| 3 | S17 | Vertebrate health and rehabilitation | 35 |
| High | S11 | Landscape-scale rat suppression | 42 |
| High | S16 | Minimizing and modifying infrastructure (less lighting, modified power lines and wind turbines, fewer road kills) | 38 |
| High | S2 | Existing ungulate fencing | 32 |
| High | S9 | Existing predator-proof fencing | 31 |
| High | S3 | Future ungulate fencing | 30 |
| High | S5 | Terrestrial habitat management for at-risk species | 29 |

New predator-proof fencing is expected to make the greatest contribution to seabird recovery. <!-- src: plan p.37 summary --> If all S27 actions are implemented, Seabirds are expected to have a 59 percent <!-- src: plan p.85, Table 26; App 2 p.A2-15; Table 12 caption on p.40 gives 58 --> chance of persisting through the 20-year timeframe. If all actions succeed, persistence is very high (69 percent). <!-- src: plan p.40 -->

> **Callout:** Seabirds do not depend on research or unproven future technologies to reach a 50 percent chance of persistence. The tools they need exist today. <!-- src: plan p.86, Ch 6j -->

:::details PTM results for seabirds in more detail
[table: Expected 20-year persistence for seabirds under selected scenarios] <!-- src: plan p.85, Table 26; App 2 p.A2-15, Table 1; p.40 -->
| Scenario | Persistence (%) |
|---|---|
| No action | 17 |
| Current actions only | 36 |
| S10 new predator-proof fencing, alone | 48 |
| S22 to S24 (combinations of predator control, ungulate control and habitat management) | 50 to 54 |
| S27 bird-focused combination, adjusted for feasibility | 59 |

- New predator-proof fences alone (S10) bring Seabirds to about 50 percent. Persistence goes above 50 percent under combinations of predator control, ungulate control and habitat management (S22 to S26). <!-- src: plan App 2 p.A2-9 -->
- **What S10 includes for seabirds:** build and maintain two new predator-proof fences per year for 10 years (20 fences in all) <!-- src: plan App 2 p.A2-33 -->, each supporting 500 adult nesting seabirds, with yearly maintenance and traps and bait boxes for rats, mice, mongoose and cats inside. <!-- src: plan App 2 p.A2-33, Table 2 -->
- **What S9 includes for seabirds:** maintaining existing predator-proof fences; translocating seabird chicks into existing fences; using social attraction to encourage adults to nest inside the fences; and yearly surveys of recruitment. <!-- src: plan App 2 p.A2-32, Table 2 -->
- **What S16 includes:** legislation and outreach to reduce night lighting; power line diverters with Hawaiian Electric; wind turbine changes in areas set by Habitat Conservation Plans; speed bumps within Haleakalā to reduce car strikes; and staff to review and update Habitat Conservation Plans. <!-- src: plan App 2 p.A2-35, Table 2 -->
- **What S17 includes:** support for the Hawaii Wildlife Center on Hawaiʻi Island to transport and care for injured Maui Nui birds, and satellite rehabilitation facilities on Maui and Molokaʻi. <!-- src: plan App 2 pp.A2-35 to A2-36, Table 2 -->
- Predator-proof fences are described as critical to preventing extinctions of Seabirds, Waterbirds and Terrestrial snails. <!-- src: plan App 2 p.A2-16 -->
:::

## Monitoring and research needs
- **Recruitment surveys.** The fencing strategies include yearly surveys to measure how many translocated or socially attracted birds join fenced colonies. <!-- src: plan App 2 pp.A2-32 to A2-33, Table 2 -->
- **Indirect indicators.** The plan gives burrow counts as an example of an indirect indicator for seabird monitoring. <!-- src: plan Ch 8, p.106 -->
- **Ungulate fencing and habitat work.** Existing and future ungulate fencing (S2, S3) and terrestrial habitat management (S5) are also high-priority supporting actions. <!-- src: plan p.40, Table 12 -->

## Island by island
- **East Maui.** The largest current ʻuaʻu colony (about 4,000 pairs <!-- src: plan p.38, Table 10 -->). Targets: 8,000 ʻuaʻu, 1,000 ʻaʻo and 500 ʻakēʻakē pairs. <!-- src: plan p.39, Table 11 -->
- **West Maui.** Currently 100-200 ʻuaʻu pairs. Targets: 4,000 ʻuaʻu, 800 ʻaʻo and 400 ʻakēʻakē pairs. <!-- src: plan pp.38-39, Tables 10-11 -->
- **Molokaʻi.** Currently 1-50 pairs of each species. Targets: 1,000 ʻuaʻu, 600 ʻaʻo and 300 ʻakēʻakē pairs. <!-- src: plan pp.38-39, Tables 10-11 -->
- **Lānaʻi.** About 2,000 ʻuaʻu pairs today. Targets: 4,000 ʻuaʻu, 600 ʻaʻo and 300 ʻakēʻakē pairs. <!-- src: plan pp.38-39, Tables 10-11 -->
- **Kahoʻolawe.** No breeding pairs today. Targets: 1,000 ʻuaʻu, 600 ʻaʻo and 300 ʻakēʻakē pairs. <!-- src: plan pp.38-39, Tables 10-11 --> S11 includes removing rodents from the whole island, and S7 includes removing cats from it. <!-- src: plan App 2 pp.A2-31 to A2-33, Table 2 -->
- **Offshore islets.** Included in the recovery footprint. <!-- src: plan p.39 --> S7 includes keeping offshore islets free of rodents. <!-- src: plan App 2 p.A2-31 -->
