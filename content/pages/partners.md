## Lead
The Maui Nui Landscape Conservation Plan is led by the Hawaiʻi Division of Forestry and Wildlife (DOFAW), through DOFAW Maui, working with a broad coalition of public and private agencies, organizations, landowners, and technical experts. <!-- src: plan p.4, lines 464-466 --> Each team had a distinct role, from deciding which species need help to working out where, what, and when to act. <!-- src: plan p.11, lines 687-695 -->

## Key numbers
- **7** | expert Taxonomic Teams, coordinated by DOFAW <!-- src: plan p.v line 152; p.11 lines 704-705 -->
- **8** | taxonomic groups covered by the plan, including the Hawaiian hoary bat <!-- src: plan p.11, lines 705-707 -->
- **100+** | land managers, scientists, and species experts contributed their expertise <!-- src: plan p.iv, lines 113-116 -->
- **5** | analytic partner organizations: USGS, USFWS, UH Mānoa, UH Hilo, and the Bishop Museum <!-- src: plan p.7, lines 571-574 -->

## Why teams matter
The MNLCP uses a multi-scale planning approach that combines three kinds of teams: <!-- src: plan p.11, lines 687-695 -->

- teams that oversee plan development and communications;
- subject matter specialists and the land managers who will carry out the plan; and
- specialized analytic teams that used custom-designed prioritization processes to work out where to focus land management, which conservation actions matter most for species and ecosystems, and where key ecosystems can be protected and restored.

Each team played an important and strategic role in developing and carrying out the plan. <!-- src: plan p.11, lines 694-695 -->

> **Callout:** The plan's strength comes from the on-the-ground knowledge of dozens of subject matter experts, built in throughout the planning process. <!-- src: plan p.10, lines 672-673 -->

## The teams

### Core Team (DOFAW-led)
Organized by DOFAW, the Core Team brought together land managers, key leaders from the other teams, and a facilitator. It provided recommendations and organizational support to DOFAW <!-- src: plan p.15, lines 849-851 --> and oversaw plan development, scheduling, and coordination of the other MNLCP teams. <!-- src: plan glossary p.120, lines 4789-4790 -->

### Taxonomic Teams (DOFAW-led)
Teams of species specialists were the irreplaceable backbone of the planning effort. <!-- src: plan p.11, lines 699-700 --> DOFAW coordinated the taxon expert teams, and experts participated in collaborative review sessions throughout the process. <!-- src: plan p.11, lines 702-703; p.A1-1, lines 4942-4943 --> They contributed their knowledge of which at-risk species to include, where those species occur, their habitat requirements and conservation needs, and how feasible management actions are. <!-- src: plan p.11, lines 700-702 -->

Seven <!-- src: plan p.11, line 704 --> Taxonomic Teams focused on seven key taxonomic groups: <!-- src: plan p.11, lines 704-714 -->

- Forest birds
- Seabirds
- Waterbirds
- Plants
- Terrestrial snails
- Terrestrial arthropods
- Aquatic invertebrates

The Hawaiian hoary bat (ʻōpeʻapeʻa), the only native terrestrial mammal in the Hawaiian Islands, is the eighth taxonomic group but did not have its own team. <!-- src: plan p.11, lines 705-707 -->

The Taxonomic Teams were especially important in six <!-- src: plan p.11, line 715 --> key tasks: <!-- src: plan p.11-12, lines 715-727 -->

1. Identifying the at-risk species to include in the MNLCP
2. Setting targets for restoring species to healthy, self-sustaining populations, using outputs from the spatial, action, and ecosystem prioritization efforts
3. Identifying known locations of populations
4. Selecting areas with appropriate habitat for each species
5. Determining the most important conservation actions for preventing extinctions and reaching recovery
6. Identifying species that may face an imminent threat of extinction in the next five years

Of the 442 <!-- src: plan p.16, line 874 --> at-risk species, 240 <!-- src: plan p.16, line 879 --> were identified as at risk by the experts in each taxonomic group, beyond the 202 <!-- src: plan p.16, line 877 --> already on state and federal lists. <!-- src: plan p.16, lines 874-880 --> The teams also recommended the order in which each group's actions should be carried out. <!-- src: plan p.14, lines 811-815 -->

[Meet the taxonomic groups](groups.html)

### Spatial Prioritization Team (USGS-led)
This team addresses "where to manage" for the recovery of all Maui Nui at-risk species. <!-- src: plan p.12, lines 731-732 --> The plan uses the USGS-developed Transparent, Flexible, Expert-engaged Systematic Conservation Planning (TFE-SCP) process, a comprehensive planning process with distinct stages, each supported by custom-built tools and involving input from biological experts and land managers. <!-- src: plan p.12, lines 732-737 -->

The work is state-led and practitioner-driven. DOFAW coordinates the expert teams and will implement the resulting conservation priorities; USGS provided the technical methods, prioritization tools, and spatial analysis support. <!-- src: plan p.A1-1, lines 4953-4956 --> The final products, recovery footprints and supporting information on species, habitats, and threats, are held in the Adaptive Conservation Planner (Conservation Planner). <!-- src: plan p.12, lines 742-746 -->

[How spatial prioritization works](approach.html)

### Action Prioritization Team (UH Mānoa-led)
This team addresses "what to do." Using a modified Priority Threat Management (PTM) process, it identified which management actions provide the greatest benefit for species recovery. <!-- src: plan p.13, lines 784-787 --> DOFAW and UH Mānoa convened collaborative workshops in which roughly 100 <!-- src: plan p.13, line 788 --> land managers and scientists contributed professional judgment through open-ended discussions on the expected cost, feasibility, and benefit of different management strategies. <!-- src: plan p.13, lines 787-792 -->

[Explore priority actions](actions.html)

### Ecosystem Prioritization (UH Hilo-led)
The ecosystem effort, led by UH Hilo, identifies remaining good-quality native ecosystems and unique community types, conservation strategies to reach ecosystem goals, and conservation opportunities based on habitat quality, the conservation status of lands, and the importance of an area to at-risk species or other values. <!-- src: plan p.v line 152; p.14, lines 817-832 -->

[Explore ecosystem priorities](ecosystems.html)

### Implementation Team (DOFAW-led)
DOFAW land managers, planners, and decision-makers have been engaged throughout the planning process. They are responsible for initiating planning, setting management needs and objectives, and turning the results of the other efforts into a cohesive plan for implementation: the "when to do it" guidance. The team also oversees a Managers' Handbook, a practical guide for DOFAW staff using MNLCP tools and outputs. The Implementation Team includes the land managers charged with carrying out the MNLCP, along with partner organizations. <!-- src: plan p.14-15, lines 836-846 -->

[See how managers use the plan](using-the-plan.html)

### Communications Team
The Communications Team develops and carries out strategies to inform the public and others about the MNLCP and its accomplishments. <!-- src: plan glossary p.120, lines 4786-4788; plan p.10 Figure 1 -->

## Partner organizations

### Lead agency
- **Hawaiʻi Division of Forestry and Wildlife (DOFAW)**, Department of Land and Natural Resources (DLNR). DOFAW is charged under Chapter 195D, Hawaiʻi Revised Statutes, with conserving the State's native species and ecosystems, in close partnership with federal agencies, non-governmental organizations, private landowners, and communities. <!-- src: plan p.3, lines 419-423; glossary p.120 (Chapter 195D administered by DLNR) -->

### Analytic partners
Expert teams were supported by analytic teams of research scientists from: <!-- src: plan p.7, lines 569-574 -->

- **U.S. Geological Survey (USGS)**, which led spatial prioritization <!-- src: plan p.v line 150; p.12 line 730 -->
- **University of Hawaiʻi at Mānoa (UH Mānoa)**, which led action prioritization <!-- src: plan p.v lines 151-152; p.13 line 783 -->
- **University of Hawaiʻi at Hilo (UH Hilo)**, which led ecosystem prioritization <!-- src: plan p.v line 152; p.14 line 817 -->
- **U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service (USFWS)** <!-- src: plan p.iv line 142; p.7 line 572 -->
- **Bishop Museum** <!-- src: plan p.iv line 143; p.7 line 574 -->

### Conservation programs named in the plan
These programs are named in the plan for their role in specific parts of the work:

- **Plant Extinction Prevention Program (PEPP)** already addresses the plants facing imminent extinction, and its population data informed plant planning. <!-- src: plan p.48, lines 2091-2098; p.49, lines 2124-2125 --> PEPP partners already use one of the plan's tools to choose outplanting sites. <!-- src: plan p.A5-3, lines 7465-7466 -->
- **Snail Extinction Prevention Program** played a central role in developing the MNLCP snail strategy. <!-- src: plan p.58, lines 2456-2460 -->
- **Maui Forest Bird Recovery Project** focuses on forest bird recovery and identified priority actions to prevent the extinction of kiwikiu and ʻākohekohe. <!-- src: plan p.33, lines 1502-1511 -->
- **Hawaiʻi and Pacific Plant Recovery Coordinating Committee (HPPRCC)**, an interagency committee, was consulted in setting initial plant recovery targets. <!-- src: plan p.49, lines 2118-2120 -->
- **Watershed Partnerships**, public and private lands co-managed to promote ecosystem health, are part of the conservation-oriented management landscape the plan builds on. <!-- src: plan p.92-93, lines 3713-3718 -->

> **Callout:** Realizing the plan will take leadership and partners: building partnerships with private landowners, communities, and agencies to promote recovery and habitat management. <!-- src: plan p.ix, lines 318-319, 327-328 -->
