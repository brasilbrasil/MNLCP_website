## Lead
Everything on this site comes from the *Maui Nui Landscape Conservation Plan*, published by the
Hawaiʻi Division of Forestry and Wildlife (DOFAW). <!-- src: plan p.i title page --> Here you can
download the full plan and a map file of the conservation footprints, find out how to cite the plan,
and browse the published work and existing plans it draws on.

## Download the plan
[Download the Maui Nui Landscape Conservation Plan (PDF)](assets/docs/MNLCP_2026-09-29.pdf) <!-- file: copy of "MNLCP 9-30-2026 FINAL.pdf"; 223 pages, about 7.7 MB; rename as shown or as DOFAW prefers -->

*Maui Nui Landscape Conservation Plan: Maui, Molokaʻi, Lānaʻi, and Kahoʻolawe.* Hawaiʻi Division
of Forestry and Wildlife. Version September 29, 2026. <!-- src: plan p.i -->

> **Callout:** The plan is a living document. In its own words, it "is subject to periodic review
> and revision." Check back for updated versions. <!-- src: plan p.i -->

The plan covers the 442 native species at risk of extinction in Maui Nui, where to recover them,
which actions will do the most good, and how to track progress over a 20-year implementation
period. <!-- src: plan p.iv-v, Executive Summary -->

## Conservation footprint map file (KMZ)
A Google Earth (KMZ) file of the conservation footprints will be offered here once DOFAW approves its release. <!-- KMZ withheld from the draft: all_taxon_footprints/data/Maui_Nui_Conservation_Footprints.kmz includes Terrestrial snail footprints and per-unit detail; see EDITORIAL_NOTES.md. -->

The file will show the recovery footprints for each taxonomic group and open in Google Earth and most
GIS programs. To explore the footprints online, use the [Conservation Footprints map](footprints.html).

Footprints are living products and are updated in the
[Adaptive Conservation Planner](tools.html). The file will be a dated snapshot; the official, current
footprints are kept in the Conservation Planner. <!-- src: plan p.A5-1 to A5-2 -->
Partners with an ACP account can export current footprint data as CSV, GeoPackage, or GeoJSON.
<!-- src: plan p.A5-1 -->

## How to cite the plan
The plan does not give a recommended citation. Based on its title page, we suggest:
<!-- src: plan p.i. DOFAW TO CONFIRM wording, place of publication, and author form. -->

> Hawaiʻi Division of Forestry and Wildlife (DOFAW). 2026. *Maui Nui Landscape Conservation Plan:
> Maui, Molokaʻi, Lānaʻi, and Kahoʻolawe.* Version September 29, 2026. Honolulu: Hawaiʻi
> Department of Land and Natural Resources, Division of Forestry and Wildlife.

The place of publication follows the form the plan uses for another DOFAW publication in its
Literature Cited. <!-- src: plan p.115, ESRC and DOFAW 2024 -->

## Related plans and guidance
The MNLCP does not replace existing plans. Hawaiʻi already has many high-quality plans, but they
are not integrated at the landscape or management level, and managers must reconcile dozens of
documents with different assumptions and scales. The MNLCP brings them together into one
landscape-scale strategy. <!-- src: plan p.iv; p.3-4 Ch 1a -->

The plan builds on:

- **Species recovery plans.** Each at-risk species' recovery target is based on abundance,
  distribution, and viability criteria derived from existing recovery plans where they exist,
  supplemented by expert input. <!-- src: plan p.7 Ch 2a; p.3-4 Ch 1a; p.18 Ch 4c; p.49 Ch 6d(iv) -->
- **The Hawaiʻi State Wildlife Action Plan (SWAP).** One of the sources used to identify Maui Nui's
  at-risk species. <!-- src: plan p.16 Ch 4a; Terms p.121 -->
- **Site-specific management plans** and broader strategic frameworks. <!-- src: plan p.3 Ch 1a -->
- **Recovery goals from the Hawaiʻi and Pacific Plant Recovery Coordinating Committee (HPPRCC).**
  Standardized recovery goals used, with recovery plans and expert review, to set plant recovery
  targets. <!-- src: plan p.18 Ch 4c; p.49 Ch 6d(iv) -->
- **Species status assessments, recovery strategies, action plans, and management plans,** used by
  the action prioritization effort. <!-- src: plan p.13 Ch 3d -->
- **Hawaiian hoary bat guidance.** *Hawaiian Hoary Bat Guidance for Wind Energy Project Habitat
  Conservation Plans* (ESRC and DOFAW 2024) and the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service *5-Year Status
  Review* for the ʻōpeʻapeʻa (2021). <!-- src: plan p.115, p.119 Literature Cited -->
- **The Hawaiʻi Gap Analysis Program (HIGAP),** an earlier species-based conservation planning
  effort that informed how the plan describes the management landscape (Gon et al. 2006).
  <!-- src: plan p.92 Ch 7e -->
